# Investment Briefing: Political Risk and the Global Equity Mix

Coverage: 10 equity markets, May 2008 to Jul 2026, monthly, 2,124 country-month observations. The Political Risk Index (PRI) averages standardised Economic Policy Uncertainty and Geopolitical Risk (first-principal-component weights 0.50 / 0.50).

## Key findings

1. **Political risk shows up in volatility, not in returns.** A one-standard-deviation political-risk surprise raises a country's realised volatility by +2.0% in the same month (p = 0.003) and +1.3% the next (p = 0.10), relative to other markets that month. In the panel VAR, which also captures moves shared across markets, volatility rises +5.6% on impact and is significantly above normal in 11 of the following 12 months.
2. **No lasting return penalty.** The same shock comes with a -0.26 pp excess return versus ACWI in the month (p = 0.05) and a cumulative +0.21 pp after four months (p = 0.43, not statistically significant). Markets reprice risk quickly; they do not keep falling.
3. **No regime effect.** Markets in the top third of political risk returned 0.5% a year versus the bottom third the following month (p = 0.83).
4. **Event type matters.** Across 30 events the mean 3-day abnormal return was -1.4% (BMP p = 0.16), but this hides a split:
   - Governance (n = 8): -4.8% (BMP p = 0.02)
   - Policy shock (n = 3): -3.1% (BMP p = 0.05)
   - Geopolitical (n = 2): -1.4% (BMP p = 0.33)
   - Unrest (n = 3): -1.1% (BMP p = 0.57)
   - Election (n = 14): 0.8% (BMP p = 0.46)
   Largest 3-day moves: BRA Temer recording scandal -14.0%; ZAF Finance minister Nene dismissed -11.7%; GBR Brexit referendum result -7.9%.
5. **A simple tilt adds little.** Halving the weight of the three markets with the sharpest rise in real-time political risk gave a return/volatility ratio of 0.30 vs 0.28 for equal weight, max drawdown of -46.0% vs -48.2%, and an information ratio of 0.16 at 1.3% tracking error, before transaction costs (12.6% average monthly turnover).

## Implications for the total fund

- Treat political risk as a **risk-budget input**, not a return signal: it predicts higher volatility in the same month and, more weakly, the next, which matters for position sizing, risk limits and option hedging, while it does not predict lower returns.
- **Unscheduled governance** events carry statistically significant short-run losses; scheduled elections do not, because markets can price and hedge them in advance. Monitoring for leadership and cabinet instability is more valuable than election calendars.
- Stress-test returns are in USD and so include currency moves. For a Canadian-dollar fund, the FX hedging decision is part of the political-risk exposure, not separate from it.

## Scenarios: what to do if...

Each scenario takes its political-risk shocks from a comparable historical episode. The panel VAR turns them into projected volatility; the portfolio is an equal-weight mix of the 10 markets held by a Canadian-dollar investor, with correlations held fixed (which understates risk in a crisis).

**Tariff escalation.** U.S. tariffs on Canada and other trading partners escalate, as in February to April 2025. Largest projected volatility rises: CAN +18%, JPN +16%, USA +14%. Portfolio 1-month 95% VaR rises from 7.3% to 8.2%; holding risk constant means cutting exposure by 10.5%. In the analogue (2025-01-31 to 2025-04-30), the worst-hit market for a Canadian investor was USA at -11.9%: -7.4% from local equity and -4.9% from currency.

**European security crisis.** A major military escalation in Europe, as after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Largest projected volatility rises: USA +18%, AUS +16%, GBR +16%. Portfolio 1-month 95% VaR rises from 7.3% to 8.1%; holding risk constant means cutting exposure by 10.2%. In the analogue (2022-02-23 to 2022-03-31), the worst-hit market for a Canadian investor was DEU at -7.1%: -3.3% from local equity and -3.9% from currency.

**Emerging-market governance shock.** Sudden leadership or finance-ministry upheaval in large emerging markets, as in Brazil (May 2017) and South Africa (December 2015). The monthly political-risk indices barely registered this episode (largest shock 0.2 s.d.), so the model projects almost no change in volatility. In the analogue (2017-05-17 to 2017-06-16), the worst-hit market for a Canadian investor was BRA at -15.0%: -8.9% from local equity and -6.7% from currency. News-based indices miss domestic governance shocks like these, so they need separate monitoring: the event study, not the index, is the guide here.

**U.S. fiscal standoff.** A U.S. debt-ceiling standoff that ends in a credit-rating downgrade, as in July to August 2011 (a period that also saw the euro-area debt crisis intensify). Largest projected volatility rises: ZAF +10%, BRA +9%, AUS +8%. Portfolio 1-month 95% VaR rises from 7.3% to 7.8%; holding risk constant means cutting exposure by 6.5%. In the analogue (2011-07-22 to 2011-08-22), the worst-hit market for a Canadian investor was DEU at -22.2%: -25.6% from local equity and 4.5% from currency.


## Election surprises, leader exits and the risk taxonomy

**Polls vs. results.** Across 50 national elections in the 10 markets, each classed against its final polls, results that went against the polls moved the country's equities (against ACWI, days 0 to +1) by 3.6 times a normal day's move on average, against 1.1 times for results that matched the polls (Welch p = 0.012, Mann-Whitney p = 0.000). 5 of 5 upsets moved equities significantly. Currencies moved more after surprises too (2.4 vs. 1.2 times a normal day), but the difference is not statistically significant (p = 0.30).

Largest election reactions relative to each market's normal volatility: GBR 2016 (Brexit referendum: Leave wins, upset): -8.7%; GBR 2019 (Conservative majority, as expected): 3.9%; BRA 2026 (Flavio Bolsonaro tops first round, upset): 10.7%.
Even big political changes move markets little when the polls saw them coming, for example JPN 2024 (Ruling coalition loses majority, 1.2%) and ZAF 2024 (ANC loses its majority, 0.3%).

**Leader exits.** Of 22 departures of a head of government, 3 moved the country's equities significantly: GBR Starmer announces resignation (2.0%); JPN Suga says he will not seek re-election (3.8%); FRA Lecornu resigns after less than a month (-2.4%). The UK's 5 prime-ministerial exits since 2010 moved UK equities by between -0.8% and 2.0% against ACWI. A resignation is usually the end of a crisis markets have already priced, not the start of one.

**Event, exogenous and thematic risks.**

- *Event risks* (known date, uncertain outcome): when the outcome matched expectations (n = 41), the average move was 1.2 times a normal day and 20% were significant. When it did not (n = 14), 3.7 times and 79%. About 45% of a typical surprise move reversed within ten days.
- *Exogenous risks* (no warning, n = 31): 1.4 times a normal day on average, -1.0% in the average direction, and volatility stayed 1.4 times normal for the next ten days, against 1.1 after an expected event. The first move tended to keep going rather than reverse.
- *Thematic risks* (building over months): in 10 of 16 theme-market pairs the market lagged ACWI over the theme, by up to 26%. For the 9 moves larger than 10%, the first month was a poor guide: no more than 31% of the eventual move had happened after one month, and in 5 cases the market had first moved the other way. The moves built up gradually, so no single day stands out. Volatility relative to ACWI was 1.09 times its pre-theme level (median).

**What this means for the fund.**

- Price *the polling error*, not the election. Hedging scheduled events pays only when the polls are close or have a record of missing (referendums, first rounds, parliamentary seat counts).
- Exogenous shocks are not predictable from the calendar; they belong in the risk budget (liquidity, limits and standing hedges), and their effect on volatility lasts longer than an election's.
- Thematic risks are slow and show up in the political-risk index and in currencies over months: they are a strategic allocation and currency-hedging decision, not a trading one.

## Method notes and limitations

- PRI uses within-country z-scores; `pri_rt` uses expanding windows so the backtest has no look-ahead.
- Local projections include two lags of the outcome and the shock; Driscoll-Kraay errors allow for cross-country and serial correlation.
- Panel VAR is identified with political risk ordered first; results may depend on this ordering.
- Ten markets and roughly two decades of data limit statistical power. Treat estimates as indicative.
- Scenario shocks are calibrated to historical analogues; projections assume each market's volatility responds only to its own political risk and that correlations do not change.
- Event dates are the first U.S. trading day on which the news could be priced, taken from public reporting.
- Election surprise classes are judgements based on final polls and seat projections; with 5 upsets and 8 big misses, the comparison is indicative. Currencies are not available for the United States.
- Theme windows are set by hand around well-known episodes, and other news moved markets in the same windows, so the thematic results describe what happened rather than isolate cause.
